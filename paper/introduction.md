# Introduction

Reflective reasoning is often treated as a natural path to stronger language-model problem solving. A model checks an intermediate result, diagnoses an error, revises a plan, or critiques a prior step; if the final answer improves, the reflective step is easy to treat as useful supervision. This view motivates process reward modeling and reflection filtering, but it leaves a central question open: should every locally useful reflection receive supervision weight?

This paper follows the main framework in the Chinese outline: Functional Metacognitive Attribution (FMA) is a reflection utility learning framework for observable reasoning traces. The framework uses structure-preserving interventions to estimate local functional utility, aggregates that utility under a task distribution, and treats the resulting signal as a candidate input to attribution-aware process supervision. The goal is not generic PRM tuning, token attribution, or hidden-process discovery. The goal is intervention-based functional attribution for reflective cognition dynamics.

The current repository adds a crucial diagnostic turn to that framework. Phase 5-7 show that local utility and structural necessity are weakly aligned. A reflective step may have a positive local attribution signal because deterministic counterfactual scoring associates it with improved outcome. The same step may still have zero measured structural necessity if the surrounding reflective graph can preserve downstream utility when the step is pruned, bypassed, or absorbed by neighboring structure. Conversely, a step with modest local attribution may occupy a topology-sensitive position. This mismatch is not a flaw in FMA; it is the reason raw local utility should not be converted directly into a supervision weight.

This distinction matters for PRM/filtering. A naive process-supervision rule would use `Normalize(FMA)` or a similar local utility score to weight reflective steps. The Phase 5-7 evidence cautions against that shortcut. It suggests a structurally calibrated signal instead: local utility should be constrained by structural necessity, sparse bottleneck status, redundancy, compensation, and distributedness. Otherwise, a PRM could over-supervise fluent but redundant reflection while underweighting rare structurally necessary operations.

The paper therefore separates three layers of evidence. First, FMA provides the target architecture for reflection utility learning: Conditional Interventional Utility, structure-preserving intervention, counterfactual matching, doubly robust estimation, and distribution-conditioned aggregation. Second, the completed Phase 5-7 artifacts provide diagnostic evidence that local utility is widespread but structural necessity is sparse. Third, real-task and downstream attempts are reported only as boundary evidence: v2.1 full stochastic validation failed its preregistered gates, and the mini downstream filtering validation failed its filtering-signal gate.

The empirical core of the current repository is Phase 5-7. Phase 5 estimates counterfactual functional attribution over 800 synthetic reflective trajectories and 2400 reflective steps. Phase 6 converts reflective traces into graph-structured representations and measures `structural_necessity` under PRUNE, CASCADE, and BYPASS interventions. Phase 7 analyzes redundancy, weak compensation, rerouting, sparse bottlenecks, resilience, and distributedness over stored Phase 6 artifacts. Phase 1-4 provide conceptual framework and deterministic infrastructure, but they are not presented as separate experimental contributions.

The resulting pattern is consistent across the repository outputs. Phase 6 reports weak Pearson alignment between local attribution and structural necessity: 0.0753 for PRUNE, 0.0523 for CASCADE, and 0.0917 for BYPASS. Structural necessity is zero-inflated: 67.79 percent of node-level structural necessity values are zero, and 49.54 percent of steps have positive local attribution with zero measured structural necessity. Phase 7 reports compensation and distributedness weaker than the initial hypothesis expected. Redundancy density is moderate at 0.3842, distributedness index is 0.2976, mean compensation ratios are PRUNE 0.0084, CASCADE 0.0000, and BYPASS 0.0152, and sparse bottlenecks account for 191 of 2400 reflective nodes.

Stage 2 is used only as a held-out consistency check over stored artifacts. The full Stage 2 set reports a small aggregate Spearman signal of 0.1628 with a 95 percent bootstrap interval of [0.0916, 0.2347]. The artifact-level claim labels for C1 rank generalization, C2 projection robustness, and C3 stratified generalization are all `stratum_dependent`, without global confirmation. This supports a low-magnitude diagnostic relation, not a broad superiority claim.

The final current claim is conservative: reflective reasoning exhibits widespread local utility, but only sparse structural necessity. This finding gives the diagnostic paper its central contribution: raw local utility is too broad to serve directly as a process-supervision or filtering weight. Real-task replay remains guarded pilot evidence, v2.1 full validation is abandoned under its current contract, the mini downstream filtering attempt failed, and readiness remains `PILOT_BLOCKED`.

This framing is intentionally bounded. The framework uses deterministic interventions and structural operations as operational proxies over observable reasoning traces. It does not recover hidden reasoning, semantic understanding, internal mechanisms, or protocol-independent effects. Its value is narrower and more useful for the current paper: it explains why reflection utility learning needs a structural necessity diagnostic before it can become credible process supervision.
