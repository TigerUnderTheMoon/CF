# Conclusion

This paper consolidates the repository into a conservative manuscript narrative. The central question was whether reflective reasoning steps that appear locally functional also exhibit structural necessity. The answer from the current deterministic outputs is mixed in an informative way: reflective reasoning exhibits widespread local utility, but only sparse structural necessity.

Phase 5 provides local attribution and necessity-score records. Phase 6 reports weak alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity`, with a high zero-necessity rate. Phase 7 refines the initial distributed-compensation hypothesis by reporting moderate redundancy density, weak compensation, low distributedness, and sparse bottlenecks. Stage 2 adds a held-out check: the full held-out set shows weak aggregate alignment, but C1, C2, and C3 are `stratum_dependent` because not all required strata pass.

The final interpretation is not that reflective attribution fails. It is that local utility and topology-sensitive necessity are different operational proxy measurements. Many reflective steps are locally functional, few are structurally necessary under the protocol, and compensatory redistribution is limited. The held-out evidence is low-magnitude in aggregate, with heterogeneous behavior across required strata.

Possible future directions include applying the same deterministic pipeline to larger trace collections, manually reviewed taxonomies, and process-supervision settings that distinguish local utility from sparse bottleneck structure. These are hypotheses for future work, not claims established by the current prototype.

Final preparation includes bibliography completion, anonymization formatting, figure selection, and readiness review. Required held-out baseline controls are now integrated in the same step-level comparison space, but the final submission should remain blocked until the remaining packaging and claim-scope checks are completed.
