# Conclusion

This paper consolidates the repository into a conservative manuscript narrative. The central question was whether reflective reasoning steps that appear locally functional also exhibit structural necessity. The answer from the current deterministic outputs is mixed in an informative way: reflective reasoning exhibits widespread local utility, but only sparse structural necessity.

Phase 5 provides local attribution and necessity-score records. Phase 6 reports weak alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity`, with a high zero-necessity rate. Phase 7 refines the initial distributed-compensation hypothesis by reporting moderate redundancy density, weak compensation, low distributedness, and sparse bottlenecks.

The final interpretation is not that reflective attribution fails. It is that local utility and topology-sensitive necessity are different operational proxy measurements. Many reflective steps are locally functional, few are structurally necessary under the protocol, and compensatory redistribution is limited.

Possible future directions include applying the same deterministic pipeline to larger trace collections, manually reviewed taxonomies, and process-supervision settings that distinguish local utility from sparse bottleneck structure. These are hypotheses for future work, not claims established by the current prototype.

Final preparation includes bibliography completion, anonymization formatting, and figure selection. The next manuscript step is bibliography completion, anonymous manuscript formatting, and venue-specific figure selection, not another experiment phase.
