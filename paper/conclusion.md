# Conclusion

This paper consolidates the repository into a claim-safe FMA manuscript narrative. The target framework is reflection utility learning for attribution-aware process supervision, but the completed evidence answers a prerequisite diagnostic question: whether reflective reasoning steps that appear locally functional also exhibit structural necessity. The answer from the current deterministic outputs is mixed in an informative way: reflective reasoning exhibits widespread local utility, but only sparse structural necessity.

Phase 5 provides local attribution and necessity-score records. Phase 6 reports weak alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity`, with a high zero-necessity rate. Phase 7 refines the initial distributed-compensation hypothesis by reporting moderate redundancy density, weak compensation, low distributedness, and sparse bottlenecks. Stage 2 adds a held-out check: the full held-out set shows weak aggregate alignment, but C1, C2, and C3 are `stratum_dependent` because not all required strata pass.

The final interpretation is not that reflective attribution fails. It is that local utility and topology-sensitive necessity are different operational proxy measurements. Many reflective steps are locally functional, few are structurally necessary under the protocol, and compensatory redistribution is limited. The held-out evidence is low-magnitude in aggregate, with heterogeneous behavior across required strata.

For process supervision, the implication is direct but not yet validated: raw local utility should not be used as a PRM/filtering weight without structural calibration. A top-tier version should test whether a signal combining FMA with structural necessity, bottleneck status, redundancy, and compensation yields better PRM/filtering behavior than vanilla PRM, length-calibrated PRM, token attribution, and heuristic reflection scoring.

Final preparation includes bibliography completion, anonymization formatting, figure selection, readiness review, and downstream validation planning. Required held-out baseline controls are now integrated in the same step-level comparison space, but the final top-tier submission should remain blocked until real-task readiness and PRM/filtering validation artifacts support the stronger process-supervision claim.
