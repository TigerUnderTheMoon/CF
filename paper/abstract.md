# Abstract

Reflective language agents use intermediate self-evaluation, error diagnosis, and plan revision to improve reasoning, but process supervision often risks treating every successful reflective step as equally valuable. This paper frames Functional Metacognitive Attribution (FMA) as a reflection utility learning framework for observable reasoning traces: structure-preserving intervention estimates local functional utility, while structural diagnostics test whether that utility corresponds to sparse necessity rather than redundant surface reflection. The empirical core is concentrated in Phase 5-7: counterfactual functional attribution, structural reflection attribution, and redundancy plus compensation analysis. Across 800 synthetic trajectories and 2400 reflective steps, local attribution signals are widespread, but structural necessity is sparse and zero-inflated. Phase 6 diagnostics report weak Pearson alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity` across PRUNE, CASCADE, and BYPASS modes, while Phase 7 reports redundancy density 0.3842, distributedness index 0.2976, bottleneck count 191, and weak compensation ratios. A Stage 2 confirmatory audit over 280 held-out traces and 840 held-out steps finds a low-magnitude aggregate rank signal, but C1, C2, and C3 are `stratum_dependent`, without global confirmation. These results support the distinction needed for attribution-aware process supervision: local utility should not be used directly as a PRM/filtering weight without structural calibration. The current repository does not yet contain completed PRM/filtering experiments, so downstream improvement remains a required validation target rather than a reported result.
