# Abstract

Reflective reasoning traces often contain intermediate self-evaluation, revision, and verification steps that appear locally functional. This paper asks whether such locally functional steps also exhibit topology-sensitive structural necessity. We present a deterministic intervention framework for Functional Reflective Attribution that separates local utility from topology-sensitive dependence in reflective reasoning traces. The empirical core is concentrated in Phase 5-7: counterfactual functional attribution, structural reflection attribution, and redundancy plus compensation analysis. Across 800 synthetic trajectories and 2400 reflective steps, local attribution signals are widespread, but structural necessity is sparse and zero-inflated. Phase 6 diagnostics report weak Pearson alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity` across PRUNE, CASCADE, and BYPASS modes, while Phase 7 reports moderate redundancy density, low distributedness, sparse bottlenecks, and weak compensation. A Stage 2 confirmatory audit over 280 held-out traces and 840 held-out steps finds a low-magnitude aggregate rank signal, but C1, C2, and C3 are `stratum_dependent`, not globally confirmed. Required baseline evidence remains incomplete because random masking, span masking, graph removal, and edge dropout have no held-out step-level prediction vectors and are marked `missing_artifact` in the leakage audit. The framework estimates local utility and topology-sensitive dependence proxies, not internal-process explanations; the current submission state remains blocked pending baseline artifacts.
