# Abstract

Process supervision for reflective reasoning requires reliable step-level importance signals, but raw local intervention utility is weakly aligned with structural necessity — many locally useful reflective steps are topologically redundant. This paper presents Structurally-Calibrated Functional Attribution (SC-FMA), a methodology that converts interventional utility estimates into calibrated supervision weights through constrained convex optimization. SC-FMA jointly optimizes fidelity to local utility signals against consistency with graph-level structural dependency patterns, explicitly penalizing redundant pairs while protecting rare bottleneck nodes. The core contribution is the Structurally-Calibrated Utility (SCU) objective, a convex quadratic program with bottleneck log-barrier constraints that guarantees a unique global solution, monotonicity for non-redundant steps, and provable variance reduction over raw utility weighting. Across synthetic and PRM800K step importance ranking benchmarks, SC-FMA achieves higher Spearman rank correlation with oracle step labels than raw CIU, random baselines, and information-theoretic heuristics. An ablation study confirms that each structural constraint — redundancy penalty, necessity alignment, and bottleneck protection — independently contributes to ranking quality. SC-FMA provides a principled bridge from intervention-based functional attribution to actionable process supervision, and its convex formulation supports both theoretical analysis and practical deployment.
