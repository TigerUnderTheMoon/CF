# Abstract

Reflective reasoning traces often contain intermediate self-evaluation, revision, and verification steps that appear locally functional. This paper asks whether such locally functional steps also exhibit topology-sensitive structural necessity. We present a deterministic intervention framework for Functional Reflective Attribution that separates local utility from topology-sensitive dependence in reflective reasoning traces. The empirical core is concentrated in Phase 5-7: counterfactual functional attribution, structural reflection attribution, and redundancy plus compensation analysis. Across 800 synthetic trajectories and 2400 reflective steps, local attribution signals are widespread, but structural necessity is sparse and zero-inflated. Phase 6 diagnostics report weak Pearson alignment between `attribution_score` and `structural_necessity` across PRUNE, CASCADE, and BYPASS modes, while Phase 7 reports moderate redundancy density, low distributedness, sparse bottlenecks, and weak compensation. These negative-looking patterns are informative structural findings rather than experimental failure. They refine the initial hypothesis that reflection may exhibit distributed compensatory organization: in the observed traces, most reflective steps are neither necessary under the reported protocol nor strongly compensatory. The framework estimates local utility and topology-sensitive dependence proxies, not internal-process explanations, and is presented as a reproducible deterministic analysis framework over stored intervention outputs.
