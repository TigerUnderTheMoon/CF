\title[Structural Labels]{Structural Labels for Stratified Audit Budget Allocation in Knowledge-Graph Dependency Flows}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Haoran} \sur{Ma}}\email{mahaoran0000@foxmail.com}
\author[1,2]{\fnm{Ningning} \sur{Wang}}\email{wangningning@bistu.edu.cn}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{College of Management Science and Engineering}, \orgname{Beijing Information Science and Technology University}, \orgaddress{\city{Beijing}, \postcode{102200}, \country{China}}}
\affil[2]{\orgdiv{Institute of Information Systems, ESG Intelligent Application Innovation Research Center}, \orgname{Beijing Information Science and Technology University}, \orgaddress{\city{Beijing}, \postcode{102200}, \country{China}}}

\abstract{Knowledge-graph and retrieval-augmented information systems expose dependency flows, entity bindings, and verification records that must be audited under fixed budgets. A scalar score does not indicate which artifacts are bottlenecks, which evidence is redundant, or what downstream records become visible. We systematize fixed-budget audit allocation around graph-topology labels and evaluate a label-guided Life-Saving First policy with Impact Coverage@K. Countries-KG serves as a clean implementation fixture. A complementary controlled knowledge-maintenance experiment uses a corrected multidisciplinary Wikidata substrate with 2000 nodes and 6039 edges. At the prespecified 25\% budget, Flat Top-K attains higher reachability coverage than Life-Saving First (0.993 versus 0.953), whereas Life-Saving First protects more uniquely at-risk records (0.972 versus 0.895). At 5\%, its small coverage advantage over Flat Top-K does not confirm across 16 discipline anchor clusters after Holm correction ($p=0.385$). Edge deletion and insertion further degrade the stratified policy more than Flat Top-K on clean-reference coverage. The findings support the audit-record representation and expose a budget-dependent coverage--protection trade-off; they do not establish a universally superior allocation policy, native Wikidata roles, production effectiveness, human usefulness, or causal identification.}

\keywords{knowledge graphs, structural labels, audit budget allocation, impact coverage, information systems, dependency flows}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge-intensive information systems increasingly expose intermediate records rather than only final answers. A knowledge-graph workflow may expose entity nodes, relation paths, retrieval passages, verification steps, and dependency links. These artifacts are useful for maintenance only when a curator can decide what to inspect under a fixed budget. The difficulty is not simply that some artifacts receive low scalar scores. A curator also needs to know whether an artifact is a bottleneck for later records, whether another artifact provides redundant coverage, and which downstream nodes would be affected if the selected artifact is audited.

This paper therefore moves the contribution away from a scoring race and toward audit-record representation rather than score improvement. It provides a systematization of fixed-budget audit allocation through structural label extraction and label-guided budget allocation. We propose a \emph{structural label extractor}, not a scorer, that turns directed graph topology into audit-ready boolean fields: \texttt{bottleneck} and \texttt{redundancy}. These fields are not reward-model predictions and are not claimed to improve task reasoning. They are reusable labels for audit records in dependency-flow settings.

The second contribution is a \emph{stratified budget allocation} policy, Life-Saving First. The policy first allocates budget to Critical Bottleneck nodes, then to Unique Evidence nodes, then to Redundancy Group Samples, and finally to Fallback nodes. Raw risk score is used exclusively as a tie-breaker within the same stratification layer, not as the primary allocation driver. Flat Top-K uses the shared \detokenize{raw_risk_score} as the sole ranking criterion, where the KG setting defines it as trace-local min--max normalized downstream impact count.

The evaluation follows this reframing. Countries-KG is retained as a 30-entity, 189-triple implementation fixture. The main external substrate is a 2000-node Wikidata scientist--institution--field subgraph with controlled maintenance motifs, fixed-budget comparisons, edge perturbations, anchor-cluster confirmation, efficiency measurements, and verified revision-history examples. The Wikidata experiment evaluates controlled audit scenarios on a real KG substrate rather than native Wikidata structural roles.

Figure~\ref{fig:overall-framework} gives the high-level knowledge-engineering workflow. Structural label extraction sits between raw dependency-flow artifacts and fixed-budget audit allocation: it represents artifacts as directed dependency graphs, converts topology into reusable labels, and allocates inspection budget by structural strata.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Attribution and Explanation}

Attribution methods ask which parts of an input, computation, or structure matter for a decision. Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}, SHAP~\cite{lundberg2017unified}, and LIME~\cite{ribeiro2016lime} return feature-level or local explanation signals, while ERASER evaluates extractive rationales~\cite{deyoung2020eraser}.

Structure-aware attribution adds graph context. Network studies show that importance can be non-local, redundant, or concentrated around bottleneck nodes~\cite{albert2000error,newman2010networks}; GNNExplainer selects compact explanatory subgraphs~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer}. Human-centered XAI further shows that explanations must fit review tasks and organizational routines~\cite{miller2019explanation,arrieta2020explainable,amershi2019guidelines,kaplan2024xai}.

These lines motivate the audit-triage setting. Salience identifies important evidence; audit triage additionally needs role labels for local support, duplicated evidence, downstream dependencies, and weak upstream signals. Entailment-tree and counterfactual methods add intermediate or contrastive evidence~\cite{dalvi2021entailmenttrees,wijekoon2023counterfactual}; the present framework keeps the audit roles separate.

\subsection{Process Supervision and Process Reward Models}

Process supervision assigns annotation fields to intermediate artifacts as well as final answers. PRM800K showed that per-artifact human labels can support verifier-guided best-of-$N$ search relative to outcome reward models~\cite{lightman2023verify}; GSM8K made this verifier setting concrete~\cite{cobbe2021training}. Process-based feedback also reduced reasoning errors when outcome success appeared saturated~\cite{uesato2022solving}. These studies provide the artifact-resolved fidelity field that the present framework uses as input.

A parallel line makes verification, critique, and revision available as process units. Chain-of-thought and self-consistency expose sampled reasoning paths~\cite{wei2022cot,wang2022selfconsistency}. Tree of Thoughts and ReAct add search or action structure~\cite{yao2023tot,yao2022react}, while Reflexion and Self-Refine turn revision steps into supervision targets~\cite{shinn2023reflexion,madaan2023selfrefine}.

Recent process-verification resources also show that step-level signals need distributional checks. Math-Shepherd studies automatic step verification without human annotations~\cite{wang2024mathshepherd}, and ProcessBench evaluates process-error identification across models~\cite{zheng2024processbench}. These resources help assess scalar step signals, which SC-FMA uses as inputs to structural calibration.

\subsection{Graph-Based Knowledge Engineering and Audit Methods}

Graph-based knowledge engineering shows how structured intermediate states can be made inspectable. Knowledge-engineering methods have long treated representation, inference, and maintenance-oriented review as coupled design problems~\cite{studer1998knowledge}. Early expert systems such as MYCIN and DENDRAL demonstrated the value of structured knowledge representation~\cite{buchanan1984mycin,lindsay1980dendral}. Cognitive architectures such as Soar and ACT-R also made internal states and operator applications available for step-level review~\cite{laird2012soar,anderson2004actr}. In information systems, related work on knowledge management and algorithmic accountability emphasizes that AI artifacts must be represented in ways that support transparency, traceability, and organizational oversight~\cite{dwivedi2021artificial,raji2020closing}.

Modern knowledge-intensive pipelines connect language models with structured knowledge. Knowledge graphs provide a representation substrate for entities, relations, constraints, and quality signals~\cite{hogan2021knowledge}. RAG and GraphRAG expose passages, entity graphs, and community summaries as evidence~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2023ragsurvey,edge2024graphrag}. KG-RAG, knowledge-enhanced language models, and LLM-KG integration expose entity links, triples, and relation paths as reviewable artifacts~\cite{sanmartin2024kgrag,hu2024knowledgeenhanced,pan2024unifying}. Recent surveys and KG-quality studies show why graph-quality signals become part of the audit surface~\cite{yang2025kbllmsurvey,chen2025kgquality,dai2025llmkg}. Similar pressure appears in long-tail KG-augmented QA and engineering-design RAG, where sparse support and heterogeneous evidence make exhaustive review unrealistic~\cite{huang2025prompting,siddharth2024retrieval,liu2025knowledgereasoning}.

Across these settings, the challenge is no longer simply recovering intermediate evidence. Modern pipelines already expose passages, entity links, relation paths, and verification records. The harder question is how to allocate limited curation budget and preserve records for refinement, update, and reuse~\cite{paulheim2017knowledge,higgins2008dcc}. SC-FMA converts exposed artifacts into graph-aware audit records for that purpose.

\subsection{Summary of Research Gap}

Existing work supplies three ingredients: local attribution identifies salient evidence, process supervision supplies step-level fidelity fields, and graph-based knowledge engineering exposes structural context. The proposed framework combines them into a representation layer that turns exposed knowledge artifacts into inspectable records for fixed-budget knowledge audit.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem setting}

We study fixed-budget audit over directed dependency-flow graphs. Let $G=(V,E)$ be a directed graph whose nodes are auditable or scaffold artifacts and whose edges encode dependency flow. A trace may contain entity bindings, relation paths, retrieved evidence, validation records, or rule-derived intermediate artifacts. The audit budget permits only a subset of nodes to be inspected, so the system must expose which nodes carry structural risk before any human review is claimed.

The main notation is as follows. A \emph{dependency flow} is a directed edge $(u,v)\in E$ indicating that artifact $v$ depends on artifact $u$ for audit interpretation. An \emph{auditable node} $v\in V_A$ is an artifact that can be inspected under the review budget; a \emph{scaffold node} $v\in V_S$ supports graph structure but is not counted as an ordinary audit target. The audit budget is $K$, and the selected audit set is $S_K\subseteq V$. The scalar \detokenize{raw_risk_score} $r(v)$ is a trace-local normalized impact score used only for same-stratum tie breaking. For selected nodes $S_K$, Impact Coverage@K is the fraction of unselected auditable descendants in $V_A\setminus S_K$ reached from $S_K$ by directed transitive closure.

The method has two parts. First, a structural label extractor converts graph topology into boolean labels. Second, a Life-Saving First allocation policy uses those labels to select audit candidates under a fixed budget. The method is deliberately not a universal scorer. It does not claim to improve model reasoning, recover causal effects, or validate production knowledge-base maintenance.

\subsection{Structural label extractor}

The extractor emits two audit-ready boolean fields for each node: \texttt{bottleneck} and \texttt{redundancy}. A node is marked as a bottleneck when removing it lowers reachable terminal-flow coverage from the frozen source set, or when it lies in a critical source-to-terminal dependency position. This is a graph-theoretic label, not a learned quality prediction.

A node is marked as redundant when its dependency coverage substantially overlaps another node. We define redundancy gold labels using Jaccard \textgreater{} 0.85 over dependency coverage, acknowledging that strict identical coverage is rarely observed in semantic KGs. The corresponding implementation threshold is $\theta=0.85$; threshold sensitivity at $\theta=0.90$ is reported in the supplementary scalability analysis.

The TF-IDF comparator is named Semantic-Similarity Baseline (TF-IDF), not a graph-based method. This baseline is included to show that undirected semantic similarity, which lacks flow direction, cannot recover structural bottleneck/redundancy labels.

\subsection{Cache locking and reproducibility}

The Countries-KG label validation script writes a deterministic cache at \path{outputs/countries_kg_label_validation/countries_kg_labels_cached.json}. The cache stores trace and node identifiers, \texttt{is\_bottleneck}, \texttt{is\_redundant}, redundancy group identifiers, downstream impact counts, auditable flags, thresholds, seed, KG metadata hash, and synthetic graph configuration. The audit case loads this cache and does not recompute graph labels. This cache-locking rule ensures that the label-validation table and the budget-allocation table use the same label source.

All reported Countries-KG and synthetic graph runs use seed 20260711. The semantic fixture contains 30 entities and 189 triples. The synthetic scalability appendix adds directed dependency graphs with 100, 200, 500, 1000, and 5000 nodes, using the 30-node Countries-KG result as the left anchor.

\subsection{Life-Saving First allocation policy}

Budget allocation proceeds sequentially. For the Countries-KG audit case, the budget is fixed as $K=\lceil 0.25|V_A|\rceil$ selected nodes per trace, where $V_A$ is the auditable-node set; this yields $K=1$ for 540 repeated traces and $K=2$ for 60 repeated traces under the locked report. If a layer cumulatively exceeds $K$, selection stops within that layer sorted by downstream impact; subsequent layers are not invoked for this trace. The four strata are Critical Bottleneck, Unique Evidence, Redundancy Group Samples, and Fallback.

Critical Bottleneck includes all nodes with \texttt{is\_bottleneck=True}; these nodes are not excluded for being redundant because a backed-up bottleneck may still require audit visibility. Unique Evidence includes non-redundant high-impact nodes. Redundancy Group Samples allocate at most one representative per redundancy group. Fallback covers remaining candidates only after the earlier strata have not exhausted the budget.

Within a stratum, nodes are ordered by downstream impact and then by the shared raw risk score only to break ties. Raw risk score is used exclusively as a tie-breaker within the same stratification layer, not as the primary allocation driver. Flat Top-K uses the shared \detokenize{raw_risk_score} as the sole ranking criterion. In the KG setting, this scalar is trace-local min--max normalized downstream impact count; deterministic degree and node identifiers break exact ties.

\subsection{Impact Coverage@K}

Impact Coverage@K counts affected unselected auditable descendants reached from selected nodes under the budget. Formally, descendants are computed by directed transitive closure from each selected node, and selected nodes and non-auditable scaffold/root nodes are excluded from the denominator. Impact Coverage counts all reachable descendants (transitive closure) from selected nodes, not only direct successors. This reflects the maintenance reality that a root bottleneck can affect a long downstream chain, and auditors need visibility into the entire affected subgraph.

The audit report also records Average Path Length to Covered Descendants, Early Truncation Rate, and Budget Used. Average path length is computed from shortest directed distances over the same reachable-descendant set used by Impact Coverage@K. Early truncation records whether the budget is exhausted inside an earlier stratum before later strata are invoked.

\subsection{Wikidata controlled maintenance protocol}

This experiment validates the proposed audit representation under controlled knowledge-maintenance scenarios on a real KG substrate, rather than evaluating native Wikidata structural roles. The corrected substrate contains scientists born in 1980 or later from physical, life, computing, and social-science occupations. Entity-only relations are extracted to three hops, duplicate predicates are aggregated by ordered node pair, and the cleaned graph is layered into a directed acyclic audit overlay. Layer 3 contains out-neighbors of Layer 2 that do not occur in Layers 0--2.

Evaluation is performed against controlled audit motifs rather than native Wikidata annotations. Each of 30 seeds adds bottleneck, redundant-pair, and degree-matched control records without exposing motif identifiers to the extractor. The primary budget is fixed at $K=25\%$; $K\in\{5,10,15,20,25,30,40,50\}\%$ is reported only as sensitivity analysis. Edge deletion and erroneous edge insertion are evaluated separately at 0--20\%. For each seed, the absolute selection budget is computed once from the clean-reference candidate count and reused at every noise rate. Selection uses the perturbed graph, while all noise outcomes and structural-protection denominators are scored on the clean reference graph.

The low-budget confirmation partitions each discipline into four stable SHA-256 anchor clusters and uses the 16 clusters, not motif seeds, as paired statistical units. Paired Wilcoxon tests use Holm correction and report Cliff's delta with bootstrap confidence intervals. Revision-history cases require JSON claim differences between parent and updated revisions. The corrected cache is SHA-256 locked; it supersedes a physical-science-only pilot cache and is not a same-graph rerun.

\section{Appendix A. Synthetic Scalability and Threshold Sensitivity}
\label{sec:synthetic-scalability}

Countries-KG is deliberately used as a clean semantic fixture, not as a scale claim. To avoid making the 30-node fixture carry the full generalization burden, we add a seeded synthetic scalability test with directed dependency graphs of 100, 200, 500, 1000, and 5000 nodes. The generator plants source-to-terminal chains, bottleneck positions, and redundant dependency-coverage twins so that the graph-theoretic truth is known before extraction. This makes the test an implementation-scalability and controlled-label-recovery check rather than a noisy open-domain KG validation.

The synthetic graphs use seed 20260711 and the same redundancy threshold family as the semantic fixture. Table~\ref{tab:synthetic-scalability} places Countries-KG at the left anchor and then reports macro F1 across the synthetic scale points. The structural extractor remains at macro F1 1.000 through 5000 nodes because the synthetic truth is generated from the same deterministic bottleneck and redundancy rules. \section{Boundary Diagnosis}
\label{sec:boundary-diagnosis}

Countries-KG supplies an internal consistency check under a directed dependency fixture, but the representation should not be assumed informative when graph semantics are weak. We therefore retain a compact exploratory boundary check on PRM800K, where the graph constructor supplies sparse topical edges rather than explicit dependency flows. Appendix B reports this route as exploratory boundary evidence, not as a necessary-condition or causal test.

In this route, TF-IDF graph edges are sparse and directionless relative to the explicit dependency semantics required by the KG audit representation.

The compact finding is that annotation-order consistency on PRM800K is dominated by a supervised fidelity field and positional/lexical cues. The supervised \boundarywstruct{} field reaches Spearman $\rho=0.611$ on the locked split. A same-supervision structure-only Ridge control reaches only $\rho=0.043$, while adding position raises it to $\rho=0.603$. Thus, graph-derived fields do not provide independent annotation-order signal in this route; they remain organizational audit-record fields rather than validated ranking predictors.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central shift is from scalar audit priority to an explicit audit-record representation: reusable graph labels, strata, downstream visibility, and structural-protection fields. Countries-KG remains an implementation fixture. The corrected Wikidata substrate adds a real graph topology, multidisciplinary entities, noise perturbations, revision evidence, and engineering measurements, while controlled motifs keep the evaluation target observable.

Life-Saving First should be read as an illustrative consumer of the representation, not as the paper's universal optimizer. At $K=25\%$, it sacrifices reachability coverage for higher protection of uniquely at-risk records and exhausts its budget inside the bottleneck stratum. At $K=5\%$, its seed-level advantage does not confirm across anchor clusters. The clustered variant removes selected redundancy waste but does not improve the confirmation result. These findings motivate exposing both reachability and structural protection rather than collapsing audit allocation into one headline metric.

The impact-coverage metric should be read as a dependency-flow visibility measure. It asks how much unselected downstream audit surface is affected by the selected nodes under a fixed budget. It does not measure human review speed, decision confidence, correctness of human judgments, production maintenance cost, or causal effect.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The Wikidata experiment remains controlled. Its F1 target comes from audit motifs attached to a real substrate, not from native Wikidata role annotations. The corrected multidisciplinary cache differs from the earlier physical-science-only pilot substrate, and the provenance record explicitly marks it as not a same-graph rerun. Sixteen anchor clusters are units from one extracted KG, not 16 independent KGs. Edge perturbations test selection robustness against a clean reference graph; they do not reproduce all ontology, temporal, or adversarial errors in production knowledge bases. These experiments do not show that the representation is robust to arbitrary KG noise. Robustness to arbitrary KG noise is not a claim of the current framework.

The study also does not report human usefulness evidence. The submission package contains a blank future human-evaluation protocol, but no returned ratings, evaluator provenance, declarations, timestamps, hashes, or reliability analysis are available. The present evidence therefore concerns structural label extraction and Impact Coverage on KG dependency flows, not human decision time or human audit accuracy. It is not production knowledge-base validation.

Future work should therefore use independently extracted KGs and native maintenance outcomes, compare multi-objective allocation policies that explicitly trade reachability against at-risk protection, and conduct provenance-complete user studies of auditor efficiency, accuracy, and prioritization. Learned role predictors are relevant only if their targets are independently annotated rather than regenerated from the extractor's own rules.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This paper reframes SC-FMA as an audit-record representation for fixed-budget knowledge maintenance. The graph-topology interface emits bottleneck, redundancy, downstream-impact, and protection fields that can support multiple allocation policies. Countries-KG and seeded synthetic graphs establish implementation consistency; the corrected Wikidata substrate provides a controlled real-KG test, noise diagnostics, anchor-cluster confirmation, efficiency measurements, and verified revision context. The results do not support Life-Saving First as a universal coverage maximizer: its structural-protection advantage at $K=25\%$ accompanies lower reachability coverage, and its low-budget advantage over Flat Top-K is not significant across anchor clusters. The contribution is therefore the inspectable representation and evidence boundary, not production validation, human usefulness, evaluation of intrinsic Wikidata roles, or causal identification.
