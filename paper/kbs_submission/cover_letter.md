# Cover Letter

**Subject**: Submission of Regular Article: "Functional Metacognitive Attribution: A Diagnostic Framework with Reproducibility Constraints for Reflection Utility Evaluation"

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "Functional Metacognitive Attribution: A Diagnostic Framework with Reproducibility Constraints for Reflection Utility Evaluation" as a Regular Article for Knowledge-Based Systems. The manuscript introduces Functional Metacognitive Attribution (FMA), a diagnostic framework that separates local utility from structural necessity in reflective reasoning traces. Across 800 synthetic traces and 2400 reflective steps, the stored diagnostics show widespread local attribution but weak alignment with topology-sensitive structural necessity: Pearson correlations remain approximately 0.05-0.09 across structural modes, and 67.79% of node-level structural necessity values are zero.

The manuscript also reports a novel boundary condition for reproducible reflection-utility evaluation. Real-task v3 validation was blocked before live evaluation by strict six-key deduplication, excluding all 14,878 candidate rows and yielding three governance diagnostics: F1, empty-alias hash collision, where 7,473 GSM8K rows share the SHA-256 hash of the empty string; F2, split consumption, where HotpotQA validation rows were already represented in prior artifacts; and F3, combinatorial key exclusion, where six-key OR logic exhausted the manifest. These findings are reported as reproducibility design constraints, not experimental failures.

The paper is a fit for Knowledge-Based Systems because it addresses evaluation design for knowledge-oriented reflective agents and process-supervision systems. FMA frames reflection utility as an auditable diagnostic signal over observable traces, while the governance diagnostics identify benchmark-design constraints required for reliable future validation. The manuscript does not claim downstream PRM/filtering improvement. It provides bounded diagnostic constraints that future process-supervision and reflection-filtering systems must satisfy before claiming external validity.

The evidence package separates the primary empirical core from boundary evidence. The primary empirical core is the Phase 5-7 synthetic diagnostic suite. Boundary evidence includes the v2.1 failed validation route and the v3 blocked manifest. The supplementary materials include Supplementary Figure S1, the governance diagnostic upset plot, and Supplementary Data S1, the structured governance diagnostic report.

We request that the manuscript be considered for review. Suitable reviewers would have expertise in process supervision, reflective agents, reproducibility and benchmark design, interpretability, or evaluation methodology for language-model systems.

Sincerely,

The authors
