# Abstract

Process supervision for reflective reasoning requires step-level importance signals that are locally useful and structurally meaningful. This paper presents Structurally-Calibrated Functional Attribution (SC-FMA), a methodology that converts local functional attribution signals into calibrated supervision weights through the Structurally-Calibrated Utility (SCU) objective. SCU combines fidelity to utility, graph-level structural necessity, redundancy reduction, and bottleneck protection in a convex optimization problem. On a controlled synthetic benchmark with controlled proxy step-importance labels, the full Quadratic Program (QP) variant is strongest, reaching Spearman rho = 0.608 versus raw CIU at 0.483. On PRM800K real process-supervision annotations, the primary positive result is instead the stored w_struct ranking signal (rho = 0.611); Ridge closely preserves this ordering (rho = 0.604), while QP and Projection underperform on that route. GSM8K/HotpotQA task-specific replay, downstream filtering, and PRM-training routes remain failed or unvalidated. SC-FMA is therefore presented as a claim-bounded calibration methodology for auditable verification-step weighting, with downstream deployment and external generalization left to future validation.
